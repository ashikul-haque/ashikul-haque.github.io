Research

My research addresses how a system stays correct and on time when the resource it depends on is contested. That resource may be the token budget of an agent working against a control deadline, or the airtime, spectrum, and energy of a wireless network under interference, attack, or competition. Much of this work targets low-power wide-area networks (LPWANs), which connect battery-powered sensors to a gateway across several kilometers, and in particular LoRa, the radio technology that dominates such deployments. More recently I have carried the same question into large language model (LLM) agents that act on the physical world. My work covers five areas.

Real-time scheduling for LLM agents. An agent that drives a physical actuator inherits the deadline of the control loop it sits in, and token generation is slow enough that the deadline is often the binding constraint. Treating the agent as a best-effort service is therefore not sufficient once its output reaches physical input and output. I work on deadline-aware token scheduling for LLM agents in this setting (RTSS ’26).

Cross-technology communication. Long Range Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum (LR-FHSS) extends uplink coverage to tens of kilometers, but LoRa downlinks fail beyond 1 to 2 km, which leaves those nodes unreachable for acknowledgment and control. I repurpose the LR-FHSS physical layer as a waveform synthesizer that generates LoRa-compatible chirps, with receiver-side reconstruction that corrects hop discontinuity, offset drift, and phase distortion. This improves long-range reliability by up to 79% (SenSys ’26).

Resilient and secure wireless communication. An attacker can silence an entire deployment by transmitting noise on the channel that the sensors use, and the sensors themselves are too limited in power and energy to defend against it. I develop physical-layer techniques that let commodity LoRa gateways decode packets while the attack is in progress. I presented the first anti-jamming approach for LPWANs (EWSN ’23), and later work extended it from a single jammer to multiple attackers that jam collaboratively (IoTDI ’24, MobiHoc ’25, EWSN ’26). These techniques require no change at the LoRa nodes and no modification to the physical layer of commodity devices, so they apply to networks that are already deployed.

Coexistence management. LPWAN deployments increasingly share spectrum with one another, and a node has no energy budget to spend negotiating for it. I use deep reinforcement learning at the LoRa network server to manage coexistence in dense deployments, which keeps the learning off the energy-constrained nodes (INFOCOM ’25, ICLR ’26 workshop).

Real-time scheduling for industrial networks. An industrial control loop is useful only if its messages arrive before their deadlines. I design medium access control (MAC) and scheduling mechanisms for bursty and mixed-criticality traffic in industrial wireless sensor-actuator networks (RTSS ’24, ICCCN ’26). In ReMix, reclaiming redundant airtime during high-criticality intervals improves deadline satisfaction from 32.9% to 85.9%.

Evaluation. I build and measure real systems. My experiments use software-defined radio (SDR) prototypes on USRP B200 with GNU Radio, outdoor deployments at kilometer scale, multi-node embedded testbeds, NS-3 simulation, and deep reinforcement learning models in PyTorch.
