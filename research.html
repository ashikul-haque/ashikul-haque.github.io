Research

My research addresses how a system stays correct and on time when the resources it depends on are contested. The contention may come from interference, from a deliberate attacker, from a competing network, or from more deadlines than the network can serve. My work covers three areas.

Resilient and secure wireless communication. I develop physical-layer techniques that allow commodity LoRa gateways to decode packets while under jamming attack. I presented the first anti-jamming approach for LPWANs (EWSN ’23), and later work extended it from a single jammer to multiple attackers that jam collaboratively (IoTDI ’24, MobiHoc ’25, EWSN ’26). These techniques improve packet reception rate by up to 100× and per-packet energy consumption by up to 136×, and they require no change at the LoRa nodes and no modification to the physical layer of commodity devices.

Cross-technology communication and coexistence. LR-FHSS extends uplink coverage to tens of kilometers, but LoRa downlinks fail beyond 1 to 2 km, which leaves those nodes unreachable for acknowledgment and control. I repurpose the LR-FHSS physical layer as a waveform synthesizer that generates LoRa-compatible chirps, with receiver-side reconstruction that corrects hop discontinuity, offset drift, and phase distortion. This improves long-range reliability by up to 79% (SenSys ’26). In related work, I use deep reinforcement learning at the LoRa network server to manage coexistence in dense deployments, which keeps the learning off the energy-constrained nodes (INFOCOM ’25).

Real-time scheduling for cyber-physical and agentic systems. I design MAC and scheduling mechanisms for bursty and mixed-criticality traffic in industrial wireless sensor-actuator networks (RTSS ’24, ICCCN ’26). In ReMix, reclaiming redundant airtime during high-criticality intervals improves deadline satisfaction from 32.9% to 85.9%. I have recently extended this line to deadline-aware token scheduling for LLM agents that drive physical I/O (RTSS ’26).

Evaluation. I build and measure real systems. My experiments use SDR prototypes on USRP B200 with GNU Radio, outdoor deployments at kilometer scale, multi-node embedded testbeds, NS-3 simulation, and deep reinforcement learning models in PyTorch.
