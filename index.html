Md Ashikul Haque is a PhD candidate in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Texas at Dallas, advised by Prof. Abusayeed Saifullah. He received a Master’s degree in Computer Science from Wayne State University in 2024.

His research builds wireless and cyber-physical systems that stay reliable and timely when the resources they depend on are contested. The contention may come from interference, from adversarial jammers, from coexisting networks, or from competing deadlines. His work spans physical-layer defenses and cross-technology communication for low-power wide-area networks (LPWANs), learning-based coexistence management, and real-time scheduling for industrial networks and for large language model (LLM) agents that read from and act on physical devices.

He presented the first anti-jamming approach for LPWANs, and his later work extends it to attackers that jam collaboratively. These defenses run on commodity gateways and require no change to the sensors already deployed in the field. He builds and measures working systems, using software-defined radio prototypes, outdoor deployments at kilometer scale, multi-node embedded testbeds, and network simulation.

His work has been published in top conferences, including ACM/IEEE SenSys, IEEE RTSS, IEEE INFOCOM, ACM MobiHoc, ACM/IEEE IoTDI, and ACM EWSN. The research page describes each area in more detail, and the publications page lists every paper.
