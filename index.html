Md Ashikul Haque is a PhD candidate in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Texas at Dallas, advised by Prof. Abusayeed Saifullah. He received a Master’s degree in Computer Science from Wayne State University in 2024.

His research builds wireless and cyber-physical systems that stay reliable and timely when the resources they depend on are contested. The contention may come from interference, from adversarial jammers, from coexisting networks, or from competing deadlines. His work spans physical-layer defenses and cross-technology communication for low-power wide-area networks (LPWANs), learning-based coexistence management, and real-time scheduling for industrial networks and for LLM agents that drive physical I/O. His anti-jamming techniques improve packet reception rate by up to 100× under collaborative jamming without imposing any overhead on the low-power nodes.

His work has been published in top conferences, including ACM/IEEE SenSys, IEEE RTSS, IEEE INFOCOM, ACM MobiHoc, ACM/IEEE IoTDI, and ACM EWSN.

Research Interest

Ashikul’s research addresses how a system stays correct and on time when the resources it depends on are contested. His work covers three areas: physical-layer defenses that keep LoRa networks operational under jamming attacks, cross-technology communication and learning-based coexistence for dense LPWAN deployments, and real-time scheduling for industrial wireless networks and for LLM agents that drive physical I/O.

He evaluates these designs on real hardware. His experiments use SDR prototypes on USRP B200 with GNU Radio, outdoor deployments at kilometer scale, multi-node embedded testbeds, NS-3 simulation, and PyTorch. A fuller description is on the research page.
